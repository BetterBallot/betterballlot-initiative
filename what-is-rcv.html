The Mechanics

How ranked-choice voting works.

NJ school boards typically elect three seats at once to three-year terms. Proportional RCV fills all three seats in a single election, so the winners reflect the range of views in the district — not just whoever finishes first in a crowded field.

The Threshold

The 25% winning threshold.

To fill 3 seats fairly, a candidate needs roughly a quarter of the vote to win — known as the Droop quota. It's the smallest share of the vote that only 3 candidates can possibly reach.

Threshold = Total Votes Seats (3) + 1 + 1 vote ≈ 25% of all ballots cast
Step By Step

From ballot to seat, in four steps.

01

Rank

Voters rank candidates in order of preference — 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and so on.

02

Threshold

Any candidate who reaches the ~25% threshold wins a seat immediately.

03

Surplus Transfer

A winner's excess votes beyond the threshold transfer to those voters' next choice.

04

Elimination

The lowest-ranked candidate is eliminated and their votes transfer, repeating until all 3 seats are filled.

The Comparison

Plurality vs. proportional RCV.

  Traditional "Vote for 3" Proportional RCV
Ballot Mark up to 3 candidates, no ranking Rank candidates in order of preference
Vote splitting Similar candidates can split a bloc's votes and lose Ranking a backup choice never hurts your first choice
Winning threshold Top 3 vote-getters win, regardless of share Candidates need roughly 25% support to win a seat
Wasted votes Votes for non-winning or over-threshold candidates are wasted Surplus and eliminated votes transfer to a next choice
Campaign incentive Appeal to a narrow base large enough to place top 3 Build broad coalitions across the district
